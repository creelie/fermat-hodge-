# Section 14, local step: reduction of the 2- and 3-parts (6 | M)

Status marks as in `even-degrees.md`: **proved** (a hand proof written here),
**checked** (a finite computation, script named) or **open**. The scripts are
in `scratch-checks/forkA/` (with `locD.c` and `lchain.py` as in `m6/`).

## R0. Statement

e' > 1 is prime to 6, D = 2^v 3^w and N = De'. An e'-element is an x ∈ Z/N
with gcd(x, e') = 1. Its order is n = 2^j 3^i e'; j = j(x) is its 2-level and
i = i(x) its 3-level.

A *local configuration* at level N is a multiset B of 1 to 6 e'-elements with
the following properties (as in `locD.c`):

* the e'-primitive part of t ↦ Σ_{x∈B} ⟨tx/N⟩ vanishes, that is, eq:charrel
  holds at every conductor c·e' with c | D;
* no pair {x, −x} and no T-pair {x, x + N/2};
* no 3-fibre move: no two distinct elements of one order n, 3 | n, in one
  coset x + (N/3)Z;
* no q-fibre move for q | e': no (q+1)/2 distinct elements of one order
  n ≠ q in one coset x + (N/q)Z.

A *local chain* is a finite sequence of moves, each adding one of the balanced
sets T(x) = {x, x + N/2, −2x, N/2} or σ_{ℓ,x} = (x + (N/ℓ)Z) ∪ {−ℓx}
(ℓ = 3 or ℓ | e'): the elements of the set that lie in the current multiset are
removed, the negatives of the others are added, and pairs {z, −z} (also
{N/2, N/2}) cancel. It is accepted, as in `lchain.py`, when the final multiset
has at most |B| − 2 elements, or at most |B| elements and the largest order in
the multiset difference of the orders lies on the removed side ("lowering").
Extra cancellations of pairs only make a chain more acceptable, so below the
final multisets are computed up to such cancellations.

*Intrinsic moves.* If ℓ | ord(x), then σ_{ℓ,x} lies in the cyclic subgroup
generated by x and −ℓx, and if ord(x) is even so does T(x). Such a move is the
same set at every level that contains x. Every move in the chains of R4 is of
this kind, so these chains are chains at level D_B e', at L_B e'
(L_B = lcm(D_B, 6)) and at every level M with D_B e' | M, where D_B is the
2,3-part of the lcm of the orders in B.

**Theorem A (proved).** If some element of B has 2-level at least 4, then B has
a local chain of at most four intrinsic moves.

**Theorem B (proved).** If every element of B has 2-level at most 3 and some
element has 3-level at least 3, then B has a local chain of at most four
intrinsic moves. The levels of R7 confirm it; such configurations occur there
only for e' = 5 and e' = 35.

*Sharpness.* The residual list of `sec14-global.md` (G0) contains
configurations without a chain with D' = 8 and 24 (2-level 3) and with D' = 18
(3-level 2). So the thresholds 2^4 and 3^3 cannot be lowered.

*Use.* A configuration without a local chain has 2-level at most 3 everywhere
(Theorem A) and 3-level at most 2 everywhere (Theorem B). Then D_B | 72
and B lies in the subgroup of order 72e', the case treated in `sec14-prime.md`
and `sec14-composite.md`.

The rest of the note is the proof of Theorem A (R1 to R5), the proof of
Theorem B (R6) and the computations (R7).

## R1. Coordinates and the box relations (proved)

Write an e'-element of order n = 2^j 3^i e' as x = (N/n)u with u ∈ (Z/n)^x and
x = [t, β, μ]_{j,i}, where t = u mod 2^j, β = u mod 3^i and μ = u mod e'.
Then:

* if j ≥ 2, x + N/2 = [t(1 + 2^{j−1}), β, μ]_{j,i}, because N/2 = (N/n)(n/2)
  and n/2 ≡ 2^{j−1} mod 2^j, 0 mod 3^i, 0 mod e';
* 3x = [t, β mod 3^{i−1}, μ]_{j,i−1};
* for i = 1 the coset x + (N/3)Z is {[t, ±β, μ]_{j,1}, [t/3, μ/3]_{j,0}}; for
  i ≥ 2 it consists of the three siblings [t, β', μ]_{j,i} with
  β' ≡ β mod 3^{i−1};
* for q | e' the coset x + (N/q)Z consists of the elements [t, β, μ']_{j,i}
  with μ' ∈ μK_q, plus one element of order n/q when q || e'. Here
  K_q = {u ∈ U : u ≡ 1 mod e'/q} in U = (Z/e')^x, and |K_q| = q − 1 if
  q || e', |K_q| = q if q^2 | e'.

*One element.* Let χ be an odd character mod N with primitive e'-component ψ,
of conductor f = 2^a 3^b e'. The standard evaluation of Σ_s χ(s)⟨su/n⟩ through
B_{1,χ} ≠ 0 turns eq:charrel at conductor f into

  Σ_{x ∈ B, f | n_x} χ(u_x) Π_{p | n_x, p ∤ f} (1 − χ̄(p)) / φ(n_x) = 0,

and only p = 2, 3 can occur in the product.

*Boxes.* Let s ∈ G_2 = (Z/2^v)^x and a ∈ G_3 = (Z/3^w)^x with normalised Haar
measures. The box of x is Q(x) = {s ≡ t mod 2^j} × {a ≡ β mod 3^i}, of volume
1/φ(2^j)φ(3^i). Z[U] has basis [μ], ι[μ] = [−μ], and (c)_* is the map
[μ] ↦ [cμ]. Put

  R(s, a) = Σ_{x∈B} ( 1_{Q(x)}(s,a) [μ_x] − 1_{−Q(x)}(s,a) [−μ_x] ) ∈ Z[U].

Then R(−s, −a) = −ιR(s, a), so ∫ χ_2χ_3 ψ(R) vanishes when χ_2χ_3ψ is even and
equals twice the left side above (up to the factor φ(e')) when it is odd.

Let 𝔖 = {f ∈ Z[U] : ψ(f) = 0 for every primitive ψ mod e'}. The cases of
(χ_2, χ_3) give:

* **(Add)** (χ_2, χ_3 both nontrivial; no Euler factor). For each primitive ψ
  the function ψ(R(s, a)) is orthogonal to every χ_2 ⊗ χ_3 with both factors
  nontrivial, so it is g(s) + h(a). Hence
  R(s,a) − R(s',a) − R(s,a') + R(s',a') ∈ 𝔖 for all s, s', a, a'.
* **(E3)** (χ_3 trivial, χ_2 nontrivial; the factor 1 − χ̄_2(3)ψ̄(3) for i ≥ 1).
  Let B^(3) = {[t/3, β, μ/3]_{j,i} : x ∈ B, i ≥ 1} with the boxes of B, and
  R^(3) its function. Then ∫ (R − R^(3))(s, a) da is constant in s modulo
  𝔖 ⊗ Q.
* **(E2)** (χ_2 trivial, χ_3 nontrivial; the factor 1 − χ̄_3(2)ψ̄(2) for
  j ≥ 1). Let B^(2) = {[t, β/2, μ/2]_{j,i} : x ∈ B, j ≥ 1} with the boxes of B,
  and R^(2) its function. Then ∫ (R − R^(2))(s, a) ds is constant in a modulo
  𝔖 ⊗ Q. It is used in Theorem B (R6); **(E23)** (both trivial) is not used.

## R2. Split functions over Q (proved)

U is the product of its p-parts, K_p lies in the p-part, and H = Π_{p|e'} K_p
is a direct product. With E_p f = |K_p|^{-1} Σ_{k∈K_p} k·f one has
ψ(E_p f) = ψ(f) if ψ is trivial on K_p and 0 otherwise. A character is
primitive iff it is nontrivial on every K_p, so

  f ∈ 𝔖 ⊗ Q  ⟺  Π_p (1 − E_p) f = 0,

a condition on each coset of H separately. A *line* is a coset L = wK_q; since
1_L = |K_q| E_q[w], every line lies in 𝔖.

**Support Lemma.** A nonzero f ∈ 𝔖 ⊗ Q has at least min_p |K_p| points in its
support.

Proof. Restrict to one coset of H, identified with K_1 × H', H' = Π_{p≥2} K_p,
and induct on the number r of primes. For r = 1, f is constant on the coset.
For r ≥ 2 let h = Π_{p≥2}(1 − E_p) f, so (1 − E_1)h = 0 and h is
K_1-invariant. The slice h_c at c ∈ K_1 is Π_{p≥2}(1 − E_p) f_c. If h ≠ 0,
every h_c is nonzero, so every slice f_c is nonzero and the support has at
least |K_1| points. If h = 0, every slice lies in 𝔖_{H'} ⊗ Q and some slice is
nonzero; apply the induction. ∎

**Lemma L.** If f ∈ 𝔖 ⊗ Q is nonzero with at most 6 points in its support,
then f = c·1_L for a line L along some K_q with |K_q| ≤ 6.

Proof. Two cosets of H would need at least 8 points, so f lives on one. For
r = 1 this is the case r = 1 above. For r ≥ 2 the largest prime of e' is at
least 7; call it p_1, so |K_1| ≥ 6. If h ≠ 0, every slice is nonzero, so
|K_1| = 6 and every slice is a_c[w_c]. Since h_c = h_{c'}, the difference
a_c[w_c] − a_{c'}[w_{c'}] lies in 𝔖_{H'} ⊗ Q with at most 2 points, so it
vanishes: f = a·1_{K_1·w}, a line. If h = 0, the nonzero slices lie in
𝔖_{H'} ⊗ Q, there is only one (two need 8 points), and the induction applies.
∎

*Corollary (lem:grid).* An integer f ∈ 𝔖 with Σ|f(μ)| ≤ 6 is 0 or ±1_L.

*Regimes.* The *universal regime* is 5 ∤ e' and not 7 || e'. Then every |K_p|
is at least 7, and a nonzero element of 𝔖 has mass at least 7. Otherwise
(5 | e' or 7 || e', the *line regime*) the lines of size at most 6 are the
cosets of K_5 (size 4 if 5 || e', 5 if 25 | e') and of K_7 (size 6, 7 || e').
Two distinct lines of size at least 4 meet in at most one point, because
K_q ∩ K_{q'} = 1.

## R3. Tops and orbits (proved)

From now on J = max j(x) ≥ 4, so v ≥ 4, and η = 1 + 2^{J−1}. A *top* is an
element with j = J. Only tops see the difference between s = t and s = tη,
since a box of 2-level j < J contains both. The *orbit* of t ∈ (Z/2^J)^x is
O_t = {±t, ±tη}, a coset of the subgroup of order 4.

*Signs.* Fix a base t of an orbit O. A top x with t_x ∈ O has a type (ε, σ):
(+,+) if t_x = t, (−,+) if t_x = tη, (−,−) if t_x = −t, (+,−) if t_x = −tη.
Then

  Φ_O(a) := R(t, a) − R(tη, a) = Σ_{tops x of O} ε 1_{σβ_x}(a) [σμ_x],

where 1_{σβ}(a) is the indicator of a ≡ σβ mod 3^{i(x)}. σβ_x mod 3^i is the
*signed box* and σμ_x the *label* of x. Changing the base t to tη, −t or −tη
acts on the four types as the Klein group; t → tη replaces Φ_O by −Φ_O.

*The twist.* (Z/2^J)^x / {±1, ±η} is cyclic of order K = 2^{J−3} ≥ 2,
generated by 3, and 3^K ≡ η mod 2^J. Write O_k = O_{3^k t} with base 3^k t for
0 ≤ k < K. Then O_K is O_0 with base tη, so its functions are those of O_0
with the sign changed.

**(T1)** Φ_O(a) − Φ_O(a') ∈ 𝔖 for all a, a'. (This is (Add) with s = t,
s' = tη.)

**(T2)** I_{O_t} − (1/3)_* W_{O_{3t}} ∈ 𝔖 ⊗ Q, where I_O = ∫ Φ_O(a) da and
W_O = Σ_{tops of O with i ≥ 1} ε φ(3^i)^{-1} [σμ].

Proof. (E3) at s = t and s = tη. The elements of B^(3) whose 2-coordinate lies
in O_t are [t_x/3, β, μ/3] for the tops x of O_{3t} with i ≥ 1, with the same
type relative to the bases t and 3t; integrating their boxes over a gives
φ(3^i)^{-1}. ∎

**No-cancellation.** Two tops of one orbit with the same 3-level, the same
signed box and the same label have the same ε.

Proof. If σ = σ', they have the same β and μ, and opposite ε means positions
{t, tη} or {−t, −tη}: a T-pair. If σ = −σ', then β' = −β, μ' = −μ, and opposite
ε means positions {t, −t} or {tη, −tη}: a pair. ∎

**K3-pairs.** For a top y = [s, α, μ]_{J,1} put y* = [−sη, α, −μ]_{J,1}; then
y** = y. If y has type (ε, σ), then y* has type (ε, −σ) with the same label and
the opposite signed box, so {y, y*} contributes the constant ε[σμ] to Φ_O. The
configuration *(C)* is: y, y* and −3y = [−s, −μ]_{J,0} lie in B (symmetric in
y and y*, since −3y* = [sη, μ]_{J,0}).

**Flat-orbit lemma.** Fix a label λ and suppose the λ-coefficient Φ_{O,λ}(a) is
constant on every ball of 3-level m − 1 ≥ 0. Then no top of O at label λ has
3-level ≥ max(m, 2), and if m = 1 the tops of level 1 at λ form K3-pairs (with
multiplicity).

Proof. Let k ≥ m be the largest level of a top at λ and P a ball of level
k − 1. Lower levels contribute equally to the children of P, so the level-k
contributions at the children are equal, and each has one sign by
no-cancellation. If k ≥ 2 and they are nonzero, the three children carry tops
of one sign ε, of the two types (ε, ±); two of them have the same type, hence
the same position and label and sibling β's: two distinct elements of one
order in x + (N/3)Z, excluded. So nothing sits at level k ≥ 2 inside P, for
every P. If k = 1 the children are the two boxes ±1 mod 3: equal coefficients
c ≠ 0 at both; the 3-fibre excludes the same type at both boxes, and counting
the types (a copies at one box and the matching b copies at the other) pairs
them as {y, y*}. ∎

So an orbit with Φ_O constant (a *flat* orbit) has tops of levels 0 and 1
only, the level-1 tops forming K3-pairs. Write Z_O for the sum ε[σμ] over its
level-0 tops and Y_O for the sum ε[σμ] over its K3-pairs; then
Φ_O = I_O = Z_O + Y_O and W_O = Y_O. By no-cancellation (and the same check
for two K3-pairs) neither Z_O nor Y_O has internal cancellation, so their
masses are the numbers z_O of level-0 tops and y_O of K3-pairs. Z_O and Y_O
cancel at some label iff O contains a configuration (C): a K3-pair {y, y*} with
sign ε at label λ and a level-0 top with sign −ε at λ force that top to be
−3y or −3y* (the two level-0 tops of sign −ε at λ).

## R4. Chains (proved)

Below n_1 = 2^J 3 e', n_0 = 2^J e', and "rem / add" lists the orders that
leave and enter, after the common ones are cancelled.

**Line Lemma.** Let q || e', L = wK_q, L = L_1 ⊔ L_4 with
|L_1| = |L_4| = (q−1)/2, and let B contain the rows
R_1 = {[t, b, λ]_{J,i} : λ ∈ L_1} and R_4 = {[−tη, −b, −λ]_{J,i} : λ ∈ L_4}
(i ≥ 0; b is absent for i = 0). Then B has a local chain.

Proof. Let n be the order of these elements. Add σ_{q,x} for x ∈ R_1: it
contains [t, b, λ] for every λ ∈ L, an element x_0 of order n/q, and −qx. It
removes R_1 (x_0 and −qx have order n/q, so they are not e'-elements and not
in B) and adds [−t, −b, −λ] for λ ∈ L_4, −x_0 and qx. Each [−t, −b, −λ] forms a T-pair with the element
[−tη, −b, −λ] of R_4; adding T at it removes both and adds an element of order
n/2 and N/2 (two copies of N/2 cancel). After 1 + (q−1)/2 moves the multiset is
B − R_1 − R_4 + (q−1)/2 elements of order n/2 + {−x_0, qx} (+ N/2 if q = 7),
of size at most |B|, with rem ∋ n and max(add) ≤ n/2. ∎

**C-chain.** If B contains (C) with y = [t, α, μ]_{J,1}, it has a chain of two
moves.

Proof. x + (N/3)Z for x = y is {y, y', y_0}, y' = [t, −α, μ]_{J,1} (not in B by
the 3-fibre condition), y_0 = [t/3, μ/3]_{J,0}. Add σ_{3,y}: it removes y, −3y
and adds −y', and −y_0 unless y_0 ∈ B. Then −y' = [−t, α, −μ] and
y* = −y' + N/2 form a T-pair; adding T removes them and adds −2y' and N/2.
Final: B − {y, y*, −3y} + {−y_0, −2y', N/2} (rem {n_1, n_1}, add {n_1/2, 2}:
lowering), or B − {y, y*, y_0, −3y} + {−2y', N/2} (size |B| − 2). ∎

**(d)-chain.** Let B contain, at one label λ and 3-level i ≥ 2, a top z_1 of
level i − 1 with ε = + and signed box P, and two tops of level i with ε = −,
of types (−,+) and (−,−), whose signed boxes are two distinct children of P.
Then B has a chain of two moves.

Proof. 3·[tη, b', λ]_i = [tη, p, λ]_{i−1}, so −3[tη, b', λ]_i = [−tη, −p, −λ]
and −3[−t, −b'', −λ]_i = [t, p, λ]: whatever the type of z_1, exactly one of
the two level-i tops, z_2, has −3z_2 = z_1; call the other z_3. The coset
z_2 + (N/3)Z is the three siblings of z_2, and only z_2 is in B. Adding σ_{3,z_2}
removes z_1, z_2 and adds the negatives of the two other siblings; one of these
is z_3 + N/2 (the sibling with the signed box of z_3). Adding T at z_3 removes
z_3 and z_3 + N/2 and adds 2z_3, N/2. Final:
B − {z_1, z_2, z_3} + {z', 2z_3, N/2} with ord(z') = n: rem {n, n/3},
add {n/2, 2}, lowering. ∎

**(N1)-chain.** Let B contain, at a label μ*, a level-1 top s with ε = − and a
level-0 top z_0 with ε = +, and a level-0 top e_6 of the orbit O_{t/3}, with
ε = − for the base t/3, at label μ*/3. Then B has a chain of at most three
moves. The same holds with all ε reversed (*(N3)-chain*).

Proof. Write s = [s_2, γ, ν] (s_2 ∈ {tη, −t}); its coset is {s, s', s_0} with
s_0 = [s_2/3, ν/3]_{J,0}. The two possible z_0 (types (+,±) at μ*) are −3s and
3s + N/2, and the two possible e_6 are s_0 and −s_0 + N/2. Add σ_{3,s}: it removes s (and z_0 if
z_0 = −3s, e_6 if e_6 = s_0) and adds −s' and the negatives of the others.
If z_0 ≠ −3s, then 3s and z_0 = 3s + N/2 form a T-pair; if e_6 ≠ s_0, then −s_0
and e_6 form a T-pair; add T at each. In all four cases the final multiset is
B − {s, z_0, e_6} + {−s'} + (one element of order n_0/2 for each T) (+ N/2's,
cancelling in pairs): size |B| − 2 if no T is needed, otherwise size |B| with
rem {n_0, n_0}, add {n_0/2, …}, lowering. Reversing all ε is the base change
t → tη, which maps the hypotheses of (N1) to those of (N3); the moves do not
depend on the base. ∎

**(P1)-chain.** Let q = 5 || e', L = wK_5, ν ∈ L, and let B consist of three
level-0 tops of one orbit O_0 with ε = + at the labels of L − {ν} (two in one
row, one in the other, rows as in the Line Lemma), a K3-pair {y, y*} of O_0
with ε = + at label ν, and one level-0 top e of the orbit O_{t/3} (in the
notation of R5, Step 3). Then B has a chain of four moves.

Proof. Say the two-element row is R_1 = {[t, λ]_0 : λ ∈ L_1}, so R_4 is one
element [−tη, −λ_3]_0 and L = L_1 ⊔ {λ_3, ν}, and y = [t, α, ν]_1. Add σ_{5,x}
for x ∈ R_1: it removes R_1 and adds [−t, −λ_3]_0, [−t, −ν]_0 and two elements
of order n_0/5. T at [−t, −λ_3]_0 removes it with R_4. Now
[−t, −ν]_0 = −3y, so y, y*, −3y form (C): the C-chain finishes. If e is the
fibre element y_0 = [t/3, ν/3]_0, the final multiset has 4 = |B| − 2 elements.
Otherwise it has six: rem contains n_1 = ord(y) and add has orders at most
n_1/2, so it is lowering. If the two-element row is R_4, the same moves with
y* in place of y (−3y* = [tη, ν]_0) apply. ∎

## R5. Proof of Theorem A (proved)

Let J ≥ 4. Every orbit has at most 6 tops.

**Step 1 (universal regime: all orbits flat).** By (T1), Φ_O(a) − Φ_O(a') lies
in 𝔖 and has mass at most the number of tops of O, at most 6 < 7. So it is 0
and every orbit is flat.

**Step 2 (line regime, a non-flat orbit O).** By (T1) and lem:grid every
difference Φ_O(a) − Φ_O(a') is 0 or ±1_L for a line L, of size 4, 5 or 6.
Hence O has at least 4 tops, and it is the only non-flat orbit.

*Two values.* If Φ_O took three values, two differences ±1_{L_1}, ±1_{L_2}
would differ by a third ±1_{L_3}. For L_1 = L_2 this gives coefficient 2; for
L_1 ≠ L_2 the difference has values of both signs, or a value 2, or at least
8 points. So Φ_O = φ_0 + 1_U·1_L for one line L and a proper subset U of G_3.

*U is a ball.* Each λ ∈ L carries a top of O of level ≥ 1, and there are at
most 6 tops, so at least 2|L| − 6 ≥ 2 labels of L carry exactly one top. At
such a label Φ_{O,λ} = ε1_{box}, so U is the box (ε = +) or its complement
(ε = −). Replacing the base t by tη replaces U by its complement, so we may
assume that U is a ball of some level i ≥ 1, centre b.

*Labels.* An *S+* element at λ is a top of level i with ε = + and signed box U;
an *S−* element (only for i = 1) is a top of level 1 with ε = − and signed box
−U. By the flat-orbit lemma applied label by label, no top of O has level
> i. At i = 1 every label of L carries an S+ or an S− element and not both
(the level-1 coefficients n_U, n_{−U} satisfy n_U − n_{−U} = 1 and each has
one sign), and the remaining level-1 tops at that label are K3-pairs with the
sign of that element.

At i ≥ 2 a singleton label of L is an S+ singleton (U, a ball of level ≥ 2, is
not the complement of a ball). Let λ_4 ∈ L carry no S+ element, P the parent of
U (level i − 1) and U', U'' its other children. Lower levels contribute
equally to U, U', U'', so the level-i coefficients satisfy
n_U − n_{U'} = 1 and n_{U'} = n_{U''}; without S+, n_U ≤ 0, so U' and U'' carry
tops with ε = −, one of each type (two of one type would be siblings in one
3-fibre), and then n_{U'} = n_{U''} = −1, n_U = 0. Inside every other ball of
level i − 1 the level-i tops at λ_4 vanish (as in the flat-orbit lemma). So
the lower-level part at λ_4 is c + 1_P, which needs at least one more top:
λ_4 has at least 3 tops, the other |L| − 1 ≥ 3 labels at least one each, and
there are at most 6. Hence |L| = 4, the other three labels are S+ singletons,
and the lower-level part is a single top: either (d), a top of level i − 1
with ε = + and signed box P (c = 0), or (d'), i = 2 and a top of level 1 with
ε = − and signed box −P (c = −1). (The script `labeltypes.py` confirms this
list at levels up to 3.)

The cases:

* *Full S+* (every label of L carries an S+ element). These are the rows
  R_1 (type (+,+)) and R_4 (type (+,−)) of the Line Lemma, each in one coset
  of K_q, so the q-fibre condition gives at most (q−1)/2 in each. Since they
  cover L, |L| = q − 1, q || e', and |L_1| = |L_4| = (q−1)/2. Line Lemma.
* *Full S−* (i = 1). The base change t → tη turns them into S+ elements for the
  ball −U; Line Lemma.
* *i ≥ 2, case (d).* (d)-chain.
* *i ≥ 2, case (d').* Here all 6 tops are in O and φ_0 = −[λ_4]. The orbit 3O
  (distinct from O since K ≥ 2) has no tops, and (T2) for O gives
  g_O := φ_0 − (1/3)_* W_{3O} = −[λ_4] ∈ 𝔖 (I_O ≡ φ_0 because 1_L ∈ 𝔖):
  impossible.

## R6. Proof of Theorem B (proved)

This is the proof of Theorem A with 2 and 3 exchanged. The tops are the
elements of largest 3-level, their orbits are 3-adic and are permuted
cyclically by 2 with a twist, the two siblings of a top in its 3-fibre take
the part that the T-partner plays in R3, the boxes are 2-adic (two children
per ball), and (E2) replaces (E3). Throughout, v ≥ 1, J ≤ 3 is the largest
2-level and I ≥ 3 the largest 3-level in B.

### R6.1 Tops, orbits and the twist

A *top* is an element of 3-level I. Put κ = 1 + 3^{I−1}. A box of 3-level
< I contains a and aκ together, so only tops see the difference.

The *orbits* are the cosets of {±1, ±κ, ±κ²} in (Z/3^I)^x. There are
K = 3^{I−2} ≥ 3 of them, and the quotient is cyclic, generated by 2. Since
2 = −(1 − 3) and (1 − 3)^{3^{I−2}} ≡ 1 − 3^{I−1} mod 3^I, one has
2^K ≡ −κ² mod 3^I. Fix b ∈ (Z/3^I)^x and put b_k = 2^k b and
O_k = {±b_k κ^r}, with k mod K.

*Data.* A top x = [t, β, μ]_{j,I} with β ∈ O_k has the *position* r ∈ Z/3 and
the *sign* σ = ± with β = σ b_k κ^r, the 2-level j ≤ 3, the *signed box*
c = σt mod 2^j (none for j = 0) and the *label* λ = σμ; we write (r, σ) or
(r, σ, λ) for short. A *j0 top* (*j1 top*) is a top of 2-level 0 (1). For
j ≤ 1 the box is all of G_2, since every s ∈ G_2 is odd.

For the orbit O_k put T_r(s) = R(s, b_k κ^r). The elements of 3-level < I
contribute the same function of s for the three r, the tops of other orbits
contribute 0, and a top of O_k at position r contributes σ 1_c(s)[λ], where
1_c(s) = 1 if s ≡ c mod 2^j and 0 otherwise. So

  T_r(s) = (a function of s) + Σ_{tops x of O_k at r} σ_x 1_{c_x}(s) [λ_x].

*The twist.* b_{k+K} = −κ² b_k = −κ^{−1} b_k, so a top with data
(r, σ, c, λ) relative to b_k has data (r + 1, −σ, −c, −λ) relative to
b_{k+K}. Replacing b by −b changes the data of every top to
(r, −σ, −c, −λ).

### R6.2 Filters

* **(Fib)** Two tops of one orbit with the same sign, 2-level, signed box and
  label have the same position: their β differ by a power of κ, so they are
  siblings in one coset x + (N/3)Z, of one order divisible by 3.
* **(Pair)** Two tops of one orbit with the same position, 2-level, signed box
  and label have the same sign (otherwise one is the negative of the other).
* **(Tp2)** For j ≥ 2 and η_j = 1 + 2^{j−1}, two tops of one orbit with the
  same position, sign, 2-level j and label do not have the signed boxes c and
  cη_j, since x + N/2 = [tη_j, β, μ]_{j,I} (R1).
* **(Tp1)** A j0 top z has z + N/2 = [1, 2β, 2μ]_{1,I}. So a j0 top of O_k at
  (r, σ, λ) and a j1 top of O_{k+1} at (r, σ, 2λ) do not both occur.
* **(q-fib)** For a prime q | e', tops of one orbit with the same position,
  sign, 2-level and signed box have at most (q − 1)/2 labels in one coset of
  K_q (R1).

The configuration **(C')** is a top x of 2-level j ≥ 1 with −2x ∈ B. Here
−2x = [−t mod 2^{j−1}, −β, −μ]_{j−1,I} is a top of the same orbit and
position, with the opposite sign, the same label, 2-level j − 1 and signed box
c mod 2^{j−1}. For j = 1: a j1 top at (r, σ, λ) and a j0 top at (r, −σ, λ),
both of O_k.

### R6.3 The relations (T1') and (T2')

**(T1')** For one orbit and all r, r', s, s',
T_r(s) − T_r(s') − T_{r'}(s) + T_{r'}(s') ∈ 𝔖, with mass at most the number of
tops of the orbit at r and r'. (This is (Add) at a = b_kκ^r, a' = b_kκ^{r'};
each top contributes at most 1.)

**(T2')** Put

  G_k(r) = Σ_{tops of O_k at r} σ φ(2^j)^{−1} [λ]
           − (1/2)_* Σ_{tops of O_{k+1} at r with j ≥ 1} σ φ(2^j)^{−1} [λ],

the second sum with the data relative to b_{k+1}. Then
G_k(r) − G_k(r') ∈ 𝔖 ⊗ Q.

Proof. (E2) at a = b_kκ^r and a' = b_kκ^{r'}. In ∫ R(s, a) ds a top of O_k at
r contributes σφ(2^j)^{−1}[λ]. The element [t, β/2, μ/2]_{j,I} of B^(2) has
its 3-box at ±b_kκ^r exactly when x is a top of O_{k+1} at position r
(β = σb_{k+1}κ^r ⟺ β/2 = σb_kκ^r), with the same sign and 2-level, and it
contributes σφ(2^j)^{−1}[σμ/2] = σφ(2^j)^{−1}(1/2)_*[λ]. All other elements
contribute the same at a and a'. ∎

When all tops have 2-level ≤ 1 the weights are 1, G_k(r) is integral, and
G_k(r) − G_k(r') ∈ 𝔖 has mass at most the number of tops of O_k and j1 tops of
O_{k+1}, at most 6.

### R6.4 Flat-orbit lemma

An orbit is *flat* if every coefficient of T_r(s) is additive, g(r) + h(s).
Tops of 2-level ≤ 1 contribute functions of r alone, so an orbit whose tops
have 2-level ≤ 1 is flat; there is no analogue of the K3-pairs.

**Flat-orbit lemma.** Fix an orbit, a label λ, m ≥ 1 and a multiset Y of tops
of the orbit at λ (for example all of them). Suppose the λ-coefficient of
Σ_{x∈Y} σ_x [r = r_x] 1_{c_x}(s) is g(r) + h(s) + f(r, s), where f(r, s)
depends only on r and on s mod 2^m. Then no element of Y has 2-level
≥ max(m + 1, 2). In particular a flat orbit (m = 1) has no top of 2-level ≥ 2.

Proof. Suppose the largest 2-level j_0 in Y is at least 2 and larger than m.
Let P be a ball of 2-level j_0 − 1, c_1 and c_2 = c_1η_{j_0} its children, and
s_i ∈ c_i. The elements of Y of 2-level < j_0 contribute equally at s_1 and
s_2, and so does f, since s_1 ≡ s_2 mod 2^{j_0−1} and j_0 − 1 ≥ m. So, with
n_r(c) the signed count of the elements of Y of 2-level j_0 at position r with
box c,

  n_r(c_1) − n_r(c_2) = h(s_1) − h(s_2) =: δ   for every r.

By (Fib) each of the four kinds (c_1, +), (c_1, −), (c_2, +), (c_2, −)
occupies one position. The positive contributions come from the kinds
(c_1, +) and (c_2, −) only, so from at most two positions, and δ > 0 is
impossible; likewise δ < 0. So n_r(c_1) = n_r(c_2) for every r. By (Pair) each
n_r(c_i) counts elements of one sign, and two equal nonzero counts would put
elements of one sign in the boxes c_1 and c_2 at one position, against (Tp2).
So no element of Y of 2-level j_0 lies in P, for every P: a contradiction. ∎

### R6.5 Chains

All moves below are intrinsic (R0).

**T-chain.** If (C') occurs, adding T(x) is a chain of one move. It removes x
and −2x and adds −x + N/2 and N/2 (x + N/2 ∉ B). For j ≥ 2 the element
−x + N/2 has order n = ord(x): rem {n/2}, add {2}. For j = 1 it has order n/2:
rem {n}, add {2}. Both are lowering.

**Line Lemma at 2-level j ≥ 2.** The Line Lemma of R4 and its proof hold with
J replaced by any 2-level j ≥ 2 and η by η_j, that is for the rows
R_1 = {[t, b, λ]_{j,i} : λ ∈ L_1} and R_4 = {[−tη_j, −b, −λ]_{j,i} : λ ∈ L_4}.
If the element −2z of one of its T moves (z = [−t, −b, −λ]_{j,i}) is already
in the multiset, the move removes it instead of adding 2z, which only shortens
the final multiset.

**P-chain.** Let 5 || e', let x_1 have 2-level 1 and order n, and let x_1, x_2,
y_1, y_2 be the four elements of order n in x_1 + (N/5)Z. If
B = {x_1, x_2, 2y_1, 2y_2, N/2 − y_1, N/2 − y_2}, then B has a chain of two
moves.

Proof. σ_{5,x_1} consists of x_1, x_2, y_1, y_2, an element x_0 of order n/5,
and −5x_1. It removes x_1, x_2 and adds −y_1, −y_2, −x_0 and 5x_1. Then
T(−y_1) = {−y_1, N/2 − y_1, 2y_1, N/2} removes three elements and adds N/2.
The final multiset {−y_2, 2y_2, N/2 − y_2, −x_0, 5x_1, N/2} has |B| elements,
with rem {n, n/2, n/2} and add {n/5, n/5, 2}: lowering. (Adding T(−y_2) as
well leaves {−x_0, 5x_1}.) ∎

### R6.6 Step 1: a non-flat orbit

In the universal regime (T1') has mass at most 6 < 7, so every orbit is flat.
Suppose now that some orbit is not flat. By (T1') and lem:grid each nonzero
rectangle difference of its function T is ±1_L for a line L of size at most
6. Let Λ be the set of labels at which its coefficient is not additive. Each
λ ∈ Λ carries a top of 2-level ≥ 2, and Λ is a union of lines of size at least
4; two distinct lines would give |Λ| ≥ 7 > 6. So Λ = L is one line,
|L| ∈ {4, 5, 6}, every nonzero rectangle difference is ±1_L, and the orbit is
the only non-flat one (another would need four more tops). Call it O_0. At
least 2|L| − 6 ≥ 2 labels of L carry exactly one top of O_0.

*Normal form.* For λ, λ' ∈ L the coefficients F_λ, F_λ' have the same
rectangle differences, so F_λ − F_λ' is additive. At a label of L with one top,
F_λ = σ[r = r_λ]1_c(s) with c a ball of 2-level ≥ 2. For two such labels with
r_λ ≠ r_λ', the additive difference vanishes at the third position, so it is a
function of r alone, and σ1_c would not depend on s: impossible. So these
tops sit at one position r*, and σ1_c − σ'1_{c'} is constant in s: either
(σ', c') = (σ, c), or σ' = −σ and c, c' are the two balls of 2-level 2. After
replacing b by −b if needed,

  F_λ(r, s) = A_λ(r, s) + [r = r*] 1_V(s)  for λ ∈ L,
  F_λ additive  for λ ∉ L,

with A_λ additive and V a ball of 2-level m ∈ {2, 3}. By the flat-orbit lemma
the tops of O_0 have 2-level ≤ 1 at labels outside L and ≤ m at labels of L.

**Label lemma.** Let λ ∈ L, let P be the parent ball of V (2-level m − 1) and
V' = Vη_m its other child. The tops of O_0 of 2-level m at λ are of exactly one
of the types

* S+: one top at (r*, +) with box V;
* S−: one top at (r*, −) with box V';
* D: two tops, at (r_1, −) with box V and at (r_2, +) with box V', where
  {r*, r_1, r_2} = Z/3;

and the λ-coefficient of the tops of 2-level < m at λ is, modulo additive
functions, 0 (S+), [r = r*]1_P (S−) or −[r = r_2]1_P (D).

Proof. In a ball of 2-level m − 1 other than P the function [r = r*]1_V is
constant, and the argument of the flat-orbit lemma leaves no top of 2-level m
there. Inside P, with s_V ∈ V, s_{V'} ∈ V' and n_r as in that lemma,

  n_r(V) − n_r(V') = δ + [r = r*],   δ = h(s_V) − h(s_{V'}).

The kinds (V, +), (V', −) give the positive contributions and (V, −),
(V', +) the negative ones, each kind at one position, so δ ≥ 1 and δ ≤ −2 are
impossible.
For δ = 0, the counts at r ≠ r* vanish as in the flat-orbit lemma; at r*,
n(V) − n(V') = 1 with the two counts not of one sign (Tp2), so n(V) = 1 and
n(V') = 0 (S+), or n(V) = 0 and n(V') = −1 (S−); by (Pair) each count is a
number of tops.
For δ = −1, each of the two positions other than r* needs a negative
contribution, so (V, −) and (V', +) sit one at each. At the position of
(V, −), (Pair) excludes (V, +) and (Tp2) excludes (V', −), so the count −1
there is one top; likewise at the other. So the kinds (V, +), (V', −) can only
sit at r*, where n(V) − n(V') = 0 excludes them: type D.
The lower parts are F_λ minus the part of 2-level m: [r = r*]1_V − [r = r*]1_V
= 0 for S+; [r = r*]1_V + [r = r*]1_{V'} = [r = r*]1_P for S−; and
[r = r*]1_V + [r = r_1]1_V − [r = r_2]1_{V'} = 1_V(s) − [r = r_2]1_P(s) for D.
∎

For m = 2, P = G_2 and every lower part is additive, so the other tops at λ
have 2-level ≤ 1. For m = 3, P is a ball of 2-level 2, and at an S− or D label
the lower part is [r = r']1_W modulo additive functions, with W = P and
r' = r* (S−) or W = G_2 − P and r' = r_2 (D). The label lemma at 2-level 2
then gives at least one more top at λ.

*Counting.* Let a, b, d be the numbers of labels of L of types S+, S−, D, so
a + b + d = |L|. L is a coset of K_q, and (q-fib) gives a, b ≤ (q − 1)/2: the
S+ tops share the position r*, the sign + and the box V, and the S− tops share
r*, − and V'. With t the residue mod 2^m that is V and b' = bκ^{r*}, the S+
tops are [t, b', λ]_{m,I} and the S− tops are [−tη_m, −b', −λ]_{m,I}: the rows
R_1 and R_4 of the Line Lemma, with L_1 and L_4 the labels of types S+ and S−.
A top of 2-level 2 has the weight 1/2 in G_0. Let p, p' be the two positions
other than r*; there G_0 receives contributions only from the tops of type D
and from the elements of B other than the tops of types S+, S− and D.

* m = 2, |L| = 6 (7 || e'): a + b + 2d ≤ 6 forces d = 0 and a = b = 3. Line
  Lemma.
* m = 2, |L| = 5 (25 | e'): a, b ≤ 2 force d = 1 and a = b = 2, six tops. Then
  G_0(p) − G_0(p') = ±[λ_D] has one point, against the Support Lemma.
* m = 2, |L| = 4 (5 || e'), so a, b ≤ 2:
  - (a, b, d) = (2, 2, 0): Line Lemma.
  - d = 2: six tops, and G_0(p) − G_0(p') = ±[λ] ± [λ'] over the two labels of
    type D has two points: impossible.
  - d = 1 and (a, b) = (2, 1) or (1, 2): five tops in L and at most one more
    element w. G_0(p) − G_0(p') is ±[λ_D] plus the contribution of w, with at
    most two points, so it vanishes: w is a top at p or p', and
    G_0(p) = G_0(p') = ±(1/2)[λ_D]. Then
    2(G_0(r*) − G_0(p)) = Σ_{S+}[λ] − Σ_{S−}[λ] ∓ [λ_D] lies in 𝔖, has four
    points and both signs, against lem:grid.
* m = 3: a label of type S+ carries at least one top, one of type S− at least
  two, one of type D at least three, so a + 2b + 3d ≤ 6. With a, b ≤ (q − 1)/2 this
  excludes |L| = 6 (a = 6) and |L| = 5 (a ≥ 4) and leaves |L| = 4, d = 0,
  a = b = 2. Line Lemma at 2-level 3 (the tops of 2-level 2 at the labels of
  type S− are further elements of B; see the remark in R6.5).

So Step 1 ends in the Line Lemma.

### R6.7 Step 2: all orbits flat

Now every top has 2-level ≤ 1. If (C') occurs, the T-chain applies; assume it
does not.

*Cells.* The cell (k, λ) consists of the tops of O_k at label λ, with signed
count v(r) = v_0(r) + v_1(r) at position r (by 2-level), and of the j1 tops of
O_{k+1} at label 2λ relative to b_{k+1}, the *u-tops* of the cell, with signed
count u(r). The λ-coefficient of G_k(r) is d(r) = v(r) − u(r). In a cell, each
kind of top (a j0 or j1 top of O_k of a given sign, a u-top of a given sign)
occupies one position (Fib); v has one sign at each position ((Pair), and no
(C')), and so has u (Pair); a j0 top and a u-top of the same sign do not share
a position (Tp1). Let n_1(C) be the number of j1 tops of O_k in the cell
C = (k, λ), and let π(k, λ) = (k + 1, 2λ), relative to b_{k+1}; by the twist,
π(K − 1, λ) = (0, −2λ) relative to b_0. The u-tops of C are the j1 tops of
π(C).

*Constant cells.* Suppose d = c is constant.

* c = 0: then v = u, and there is no j0 top (a j0 top of sign σ at r makes
  v(r), hence u(r), of sign σ, against (Tp1)).
* c ≠ 0, normalised to c > 0 by reversing all signs in the cell: every
  position needs a positive contribution, and these come only from j0 tops of
  sign + (one position p_0), j1 tops of sign + (one position p_1) and u-tops
  of sign − (one position q_u), so the three kinds sit at the three positions.
  (Tp1) excludes u-tops of sign + at p_0, so there are exactly c j0 tops, all
  at p_0. A u-top of sign + sits where v > 0, at p_1, and a top of O_k of sign
  − sits where u < 0, at q_u, and has 2-level 1 (Tp1). At p_1 and q_u,
  a_1 − a_u = c = b_u − b_1, where a and b count the kinds of sign + and −; so
  a_1 + b_1 = a_u + b_u.

In both cases n_1(C) = n_1(π(C)). A constant cell with u = 0 or v_1 = 0 is
empty. In a constant cell with one j1 top and one u-top, the u-top has the
position and the sign of the j1 top (c = 0), or the opposite sign at another
position, and then the cell has one j0 top (c ≠ 0).

*Defects.* By (T2') and lem:grid each G_k(r) − G_k(r') is 0 or ±1_L for a
line L, and by the two-values argument of R5, Step 2,

  G_k(r) = g_k + ε_k [r = ρ_k] 1_{L_k},   ε_k ∈ {0, ±1}.

Let 𝒟 = {k : ε_k ≠ 0} and ℓ_k = |L_k| ≥ 4. The cells (k, λ) with k ∈ 𝒟 and
λ ∈ L_k are *defective*, with d = c + ε_k[r = ρ_k]; all other cells are
constant. In the universal regime 𝒟 = ∅. For a defective cell, normalised to
ε_k = +1:

* a cell with a single top is of type α (a top of O_k at (ρ_k, +), α0 or α1
  by its 2-level) or of type β (a u-top at (ρ_k, −)), since
  ±[r = p] = c + [r = ρ_k] forces c = 0 and p = ρ_k;
* if u = 0, the cell is α0, α1, or A: a j0 top and a j1 top, both of sign −,
  at the two positions other than ρ_k. Indeed d = v, and each sign comes from
  two kinds at one position each, which excludes c ≥ 1 and c ≤ −2; c = 0 is
  type α, and c = −1 puts the two kinds of sign − at the two other positions,
  with one top each, and nothing at ρ_k;
* if v_1 = 0, the cell is α0, β, or B': a u-top of sign + and a j0 top of sign
  − at the two positions other than ρ_k. Indeed the positive kinds are j0 tops
  of sign + and u-tops of sign −, the negative ones j0 tops of sign − and
  u-tops of sign +, which again excludes c ≥ 1 and c ≤ −2. For c = 0, at
  r ≠ ρ_k a nonzero v_0(r) = u(r) would put a j0 top and a u-top of one sign at
  r (Tp1), and at ρ_k, v_0 − u = 1 with v_0 and u not of one sign leaves α0 or
  β. For c = −1 the two negative kinds sit at the two positions other than
  ρ_k, one top each, and the positive kinds could then only sit at ρ_k
  ((Pair), (Tp1)), where d = 0 excludes them;
* if u = v_1 = 0, the cell is α0.

*Supports.* For k ∈ 𝒟 each of the ℓ_k defective cells of k contains a top.
Let S_k be the set of these tops, A_k the set of tops of O_k and B_k ⊂ A_k its
j1 tops. Then S_k ⊂ A_k ∪ B_{k+1} and |S_k| ≥ ℓ_k ≥ 4. For k, k' ∈ 𝒟 with
k' ∉ {k − 1, k, k + 1} the sets A_k ∪ B_{k+1} and A_{k'} ∪ B_{k'+1} are
disjoint, which needs 8 tops. So 𝒟 ⊂ {k, k + 1} for some k, or K = 3 and
𝒟 = Z/3. Let N_1(k) = |B_k|; summing n_1(C) − n_1(π(C)) over the cells of k
gives N_1(k) − N_1(k + 1), which is 0 for k ∉ 𝒟. Let T_0 and T_1 be the
numbers of j0 and j1 tops, T = T_0 + T_1 ≤ 6.

### R6.8 Step 3: the shape of 𝒟

**𝒟 = ∅** (this includes the universal regime). n_1 is constant along each
π-orbit of cells, and the π-orbit of a cell has K·ord(γ) cells, where
γ = −2^K ∈ U. If there is a j1 top, n_1 ≥ 1 on a whole π-orbit, so
K·ord(γ) ≤ 6: K = 3 and γ² = 64 ≡ 1 mod e', so e' | 63, e' = 7 and
ord(γ) = 2 (γ = 1 would need e' | 9). Then each of the six cells of the
π-orbit has one j1 top, there is no j0 top, every cell has c = 0, and v = u:
the j1 top of π(C) has the position and sign of that of C. Around the π-orbit
the twist is passed twice, so the j1 top of (0, λ) at (r, σ) forces one at
(r − 1, −σ) in (0, γλ) and one at (r − 2, σ) in (0, γ²λ) = (0, λ), which has
only one. If there is no j1 top, every cell has c = 0 (c ≠ 0 needs j1 tops)
and v = u = 0, so B has no top: impossible.

**K = 3, 𝒟 = Z/3.** A j0 top lies in one of the sets A_k ∪ B_{k+1} and a j1
top in two, so 12 ≤ Σ|S_k| ≤ T_0 + 2T_1 ≤ 12. So T_0 = 0, T_1 = 6,
|S_k| = ℓ_k = 4, and every defective cell has one top: the tops of O_k are at
(ρ_k, ε_k) and those of O_{k+1} at (ρ_k, −ε_k) relative to b_{k+1}. Comparing
the two descriptions of O_{k+1} gives ρ_{k+1} = ρ_k and ε_{k+1} = −ε_k for
k = 0, 1. For k = 2, the tops of O_3 = O_0 are at (ρ_0, −ε_2) = (ρ_0, −ε_0)
relative to b_3, that is at (ρ_0 − 1, ε_0) relative to b_0, while they are at
(ρ_0, ε_0). Impossible.

**𝒟 = {k}.** N_1 is constant, so T_1 = K a_1 with a_1 = N_1(k).

* a_1 = 0 (in particular when K ≥ 9): every cell has u = v_1 = 0, so the
  defective cells of k are α0, and they give ℓ_k j0 tops of O_k at
  (ρ_k, ε_k) with labels in the line L_k, against (q-fib), since
  ℓ_k > (q − 1)/2.
* K = 3, a_1 = 2: T_0 = 0, so S_k = A_k ∪ B_{k+1} has four elements,
  ℓ_k = 4, and every defective cell of k has one top: the tops of O_k are at
  (ρ_k, ε_k) and those of O_{k+1} at (ρ_k, −ε_k). The cells of k + 1 and
  k + 2 are constant with c = 0 (there is no j0 top), so v = u: the tops of
  O_{k+2}, and the tops of O_{k+3} = O_k relative to b_{k+3}, are at
  (ρ_k, −ε_k), that is at (ρ_k − 1, ε_k) relative to b_k. Impossible.
* K = 3, a_1 = 1: let x, y, z be the j1 tops of O_k, O_{k+1}, O_{k+2}, and
  (ρ, ε) = (ρ_k, ε_k). A defective cell of k containing neither x nor y has
  u = v_1 = 0, so it is α0, and by (q-fib) there are at most (q − 1)/2 of
  them. As ℓ_k ≥ 4, this forces ℓ_k = 4, exactly two α0 cells, and x and y in
  two further defective cells D_x and D_y. D_x has u = 0, so it is α1 (x at
  (ρ, ε)) or A (x of sign −ε, with a j0 top); D_y has v_1 = 0, so it is β (y at
  (ρ, −ε)) or B' (y of sign ε, with a j0 top). With z this is at least five
  tops, so A and B' do not occur together.
  The cells of k + 1 and k + 2 are constant. The cell of y has a u-top, which
  is z, and the cell of z has one, which is x relative to b_{k+3}. In each of
  the two cells the u-top keeps the position and the sign of the j1 top, or
  the cell has one more j0 top and the u-top changes its sign and its
  position; passing from b_{k+3} to b_k changes the sign and lowers the
  position by 1.
  - α1 and β: five tops, so at most one of the two cells has a j0 top. If
    neither has, x is at (ρ − 1, ε); if one has, x has the sign −ε. Both
    contradict x at (ρ, ε).
  - α1 and B': six tops, so neither cell has a j0 top, and y of sign ε gives
    x the sign −ε, against α1.
  - A and β: six tops, and y of sign −ε gives x the sign ε, against A.

**𝒟 = {k, k + 1}.** N_1(k') = a_1 for k' ≠ k + 1 and N_1(k + 1) = b_1, so
T_1 = (K − 1)a_1 + b_1. Since S_k ∩ S_{k+1} ⊂ B_{k+1},

* a_1 ≥ 1: then K = 3 (for K ≥ 9, 8a_1 ≤ 4), and (a_1, b_1) is (2, 2),
