# A repair of the F2-to-AB implication

This is a proof of an implication **assuming F2**. It does not prove F2,
construct the missing Mumford cycles, or solve the Hodge conjecture.

Use rational cohomology. Let R(A) be the graded subalgebra generated by
divisors and pullbacks of Weil classes, as in Definition 3.6 of the
manuscript. Let Alg be the subspace of algebraic cohomology classes.

## What needs repair

In the proof labelled `thm:f2isab`, choosing g with ge ≠ e and then
replacing g by g² is not a contradiction if ge = −e: the replacement can
fix e. The same paragraph says that F2 makes every degree-twelve Hodge
class algebraic, although the argument there only treats classes outside R.
Neither sentence is needed. The following proof avoids both.

## The symplectic invariance used

Fix a generic Mumford fourfold X with End⁰(X) = Q, put V = H¹(X), and
write S = Sp(V, ψ). For an abelian variety Y, take its X-isotypic part,
so H¹(Y) = (V ⊗ Q_Y) ⊕ H¹(Y′), up to isogeny. Let S act on V and
trivially on the other factors. This action on cohomology is compatible
with homomorphism pullbacks and exterior products.

Every divisor is S-invariant: there is no mixed divisor between the
X-isotypic part and Y′; on powers of X the invariant alternating forms
are ψ times symmetric forms on the multiplicity space. This follows
from End_Hdg(V) = Q and the polarisation.

Every Weil generator is also S-invariant. An imaginary quadratic action
on an abelian variety preserves its X-isotypic part. On a complex
eigenspace, that part is V ⊗ Q_σ. The Weil generator is in the top
exterior power of the entire eigenspace. The action on its determinant
is (det V)^(dim Q_σ), which is trivial on S. The complementary isotypic
part is acted on trivially. Pullback along a homomorphism preserves this
invariance. Therefore R(Y) is pointwise fixed by S.

For B = X × X the S-invariants are generated by divisor classes, by the
first fundamental theorem for the symplectic group. A generic Mumford
fourfold has a rational Hodge class e ∈ Hdg²(B) outside that divisor
algebra (Mumford; Moonen–Zarhin). Thus e is not S-invariant. In particular
there is g ∈ S(Q) with ge ≠ e; rational points of the split symplectic
group are Zariski dense. No assertion that e is algebraic is used here.

## The affine difference construction

Assume F2: every homogeneous rational Hodge class outside R(Y), for
every abelian Y, is algebraic. Take β ∈ Hdgᵖ(A), 0 ≤ p ≤ dim A.
If β is outside R(A), F2 already proves its algebraicity. Otherwise
β ∈ R(A). Choose ample divisor classes a on A and b on B and put
Y = A × B. Define the two classes of codimension p + 2

    η = aᵖ ⊠ e,        δ = β ⊠ b².

The class aᵖ is nonzero and is S-invariant, whereas e is not invariant.
Consequently η is not S-invariant and hence η ∉ R(Y). The class δ
belongs to R(Y). As R(Y) is a vector subspace, η + δ also lies outside
R(Y): membership would imply η = (η + δ) − δ ∈ R(Y).

Both η and η + δ are Hodge classes. By F2, both are algebraic. Their
difference δ is algebraic. Multiplying δ by pr_B*(b⁶), then pushing
forward along pr_A, gives

    (pr_A)_* (δ · pr_B^*(b⁶)) = (∫_B b⁸) β.

The integer ∫_B b⁸ is strictly positive since dim B = 8 and b is ample.
Thus β is algebraic
with rational coefficients. This proves AB. Conversely AB implies F2
by its definition.

No Weil-algebraicity theorem, algebraicity of a Kuga–Satake correspondence,
or algebraicity theorem for arbitrary K3 products enters this argument.
The Mumford representation and symplectic invariant theorem are established
inputs, not replaced by a claim of computational certification.

## Consequence and limit

F2 ⇔ AB is a reduction; together with the direct correspondence argument
HC ⇔ (AB and F3′), it gives HC ⇔ (F2 and F3′). It does not turn either
F2 or F3′ into a theorem. Assuming that the two classes η and η + δ have
cycles for every β is precisely where the full F2 hypothesis is used.

The accompanying Lean file checks only the affine difference lemma and
elementary subspace implications. It does not formalise these geometric
inputs or the complete reduction in the category of varieties.
